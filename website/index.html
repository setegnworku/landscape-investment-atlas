Why this matters

The landscape investment gap

⚠

The narrative is upside down

In most programs we say: "improve food security and livelihoods while protecting the environment." Rarely: "protect and manage landscapes in order to secure food, water, and livelihoods."

It's not about which comes first — it's about what is the basis of what. People cannot eat if the planet is not healthy enough.

📉

High cost of inaction

Land degradation, lost fisheries, deforestation, and water decline cause enormous economic losses — yet funding for natural resource management stays fragmented and small.

🔀

Fragmented finance

Climate, biodiversity, water, agriculture, and restoration money often targets the same landscape — but through separate projects, with no shared way to compare opportunities.

⏳

Time mismatch

Restoring degraded land takes years or decades. Donors and investors often want rapid impact — while the real returns from protection and restoration accumulate slowly.

"We keep telling the world how serious land degradation is and wait to see what happens. What if instead we showed investible opportunities — concretely, what is in it for investors, governments, and communities?"

What is the Landscape Investment Atlas?

Think of a landscape like a company balance sheet. It has assets (forests, farms, rivers, communities, governance). Those assets produce returns (food, water, carbon, jobs, biodiversity). The Atlas helps answer: What do we have? What's at risk? What should we invest in? Who should pay?

Core metrics

How we measure landscape investment

L-ROI

Landscape Return on Investment
Value Protected + Value Created ÷ Investment

Value Protected = losses avoided (e.g. keeping a fishery alive, preventing soil erosion).
Value Created = new benefits from restoration or better management.
Many conservation returns come from avoiding loss — not just creating something new.

Example — Lake Victoria fisheries L-ROI: 3.0 – 8.0×

MDI

Multifunctionality Diversity Index

Not all landscapes should maximize one thing (e.g. carbon only). MDI measures how balanced returns are across water, biodiversity, food, livelihoods, and governance.

Cost of Inaction

Value at Risk

If degradation continues, what do we lose? This makes the urgency case for investment — especially for protection and enabling actions that don't show quick "new output" but prevent huge future losses.

Potential loss without action US$ 600M – 1.8B
Strategic investment US$ 80M – 180M

Looking ahead

A system to direct capital where it matters most

The Landscape Investment Atlas aims to develop a conservation narrative (CN) and operational framework that attracts donors, governments, communities, payment for ecosystem services, biodiversity finance, private sector, and philanthropy — to invest in multifunctional landscapes.

  • Shift from "problem stories" to investible opportunities
  • Make avoided losses as visible as new benefits
  • Coordinate finance across sectors on the same evidence base
  • Support pilot investible landscape plans in Living Landscapes
  • Build a digital platform — incrementally, with communities and investors

This is a co-development effort. Once the concept is shaped, a team can take it forward — from research and pilots to a full Atlas platform.
